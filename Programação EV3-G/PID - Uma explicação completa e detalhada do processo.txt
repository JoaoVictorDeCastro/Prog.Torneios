---------------------------------------------- Proporcional controller -------------------------------------------------

*Imagen for a moment that we are programming for a self drive car and we wanto to have it stop at a stop sign*
- We can do this by changing its speed as a function of the distance it has yet to travel at 50m away the car 
perceives that. We can design our controller to change the speed of the car in km/h equal to the distance to the stop 
sign or error in meters.

At 50m away the car will set its speed to 50 km/h
At 40m away the car will set its speed to 40 km/h
At 30m away the car will set its speed to 30 km/h
At 0m away the car will set its speed to 0 km/h

This is called proporcional controller where we link the speed of the car to the error of our current position

*The proporcional controller doesn't always work*
- Imagine if the stop sign was replaced by another car traveling in the same direction moving at 20 km/h n our program
aims to follow that car by staying in a safe following distance.

When our car are 50m away we acellerate to 50 km/h, so far, so good. But there's a problem once we approach 20m from
the target distance we are traveling at 20 km/h which os only just enough to stay with the in front but we have this 
constant error of 20m and this is called a steady state error (erro de estado estacionário) and if we stay with the 
proporcional controller, we will never by closer than the currenty distance, because both car are travelling at the 
same speed, so the distance will be the same until one car change his velocity. 

To fix this we add an integrator!
----------------------------------------------- Integrator controller -------------------------------------------------

The integrator pathway keeps a running total of the error of the car so as long as there is an error then the 
integrator will take over the proporcional path of the controller (Because of this, we say that integrator is for 
really small errors). A bigger error will make our velocity bigger, so our speed will be bigger than se distance + the
speed of the target car.

But now we have the third problem! Depending on how our car reached the target zone behind the leading car we may 
overshoot the destination giving it a wobble effect a big wobble in our case can risk collinding with the car in front

To solve this we add a third path to our controller the derivative!
------------------------------------------------ Derivate controller --------------------------------------------------

The derivative pathway eases the approach to the target which means that it smoothens the wobble of the proprotional n 
integral paths and control engineers will tweak the weightings.
